The story

I was broke in the one place where my life was supposed to change.

When I was 16, I walked into a bank and asked questions about investments, hoping to find a way to start building wealth. The customer care reps didn’t take me seriously.

She looked at me like I wasn’t even there. At first, I thought maybe she hadn’t heard me. So I said it again.

This time she looked up, held my eyes for a second, and then turned back to her screen and carried on as if I had never spoken.

Right then, I understood. She didn’t think I was worth her time.

I felt my chest tighten. I felt small. Smaller than I had ever felt in my life. It was as if every dream I had carried into that room, every hope towards my financial freedom, had just been dismissed with one cold look.

I walked out of there humiliated. Disappointed. Feeling like my whole world had crumbled in a matter of seconds. And that was the day I made a decision.

Then came 2016. I was rounding up my internship at NNPC in Abuja. You would think being in a place like that meant I had money. Far from it. My salary was ₦13,300.

Every day, I would step into the lift and see people holding bundles of cash so casually. Some carried it in big nylon bags, straight from withdrawing. When my madam sent me to withdraw money for her, in chunks, I saw it even more: people walking around with see-through nylon, packed with ₦500 and ₦1,000 notes, as much as ₦500k, ₦1m.

It opened my eyes to how people were making money. Then I would go back to my desk very hungry. None of it made sense to me, and it started leading me to questions.

I couldn’t even afford the cafeteria. If I used my transport money there, I would miss the bus. I was a dedicated follower of the staff bus because the rate was subsidised. It was the only way I could afford to get to work. So I avoided the cafeteria like a plague, because the food there was expensive. I would cross the street near a mechanic workshop under a tree, where one aboki sold biscuits. On the days I could afford it, I bought biscuits and pure water, just so I wouldn’t go hungry, while most interns walked into the cafeteria casually.

Then I tried learning Forex. The agents said I needed $100 for the lessons and another $200 to start trading.

I was still trying hard to keep myself fed in the office on biscuits and pure water, and here they were asking me for $300. That was when I knew I had to find another way. I did not want to die of hunger and poverty.

The financial system was designed for people who already had money, not for people like me at the time who were desperately trying to get some of it. At least, that’s what I believed at the time.

So I went looking for my own way out.

One day at the office during my NNPC internship, I heard a radio jingle advertising an event about cryptocurrency and digital currencies. I couldn’t attend because it was during working hours, but that announcement introduced me to something that would change the direction of my life.

I started learning Bitcoin, Ethereum, Dash, Monero, and the wider crypto industry, and how to actively invest and profit from them. I became so immersed that I got a masters degree in blockchain and other digital currency. I started writing about digital finance across Africa for major crypto news publications, and even got to interview celebrities, the likes of Akon of Konvict Music, who was working on a cryptocurrency project of his own at the time.

But the more I learned, the more I noticed something troubling: so many Nigerians were struggling financially. Some of the statistics I worked on from recognised bodies like the United Nations (UN) and International Monetary Fund (IMF), on financial literacy in Nigeria and Africa, felt like ridicule. In fact, I could hardly believe them. The lack of access to the right financial knowledge was the major problem.

I knew what it felt like to want to invest but not know where to start. I knew what it felt like to have questions but not know who to ask. And I knew what it felt like to watch opportunities pass me by because I didn’t understand how they worked.

So I expanded beyond crypto. I invested in courses to understand stocks, ETFs, mutual funds, bonds and other traditional financial instruments. I wanted to understand how these markets worked, how they compared with crypto, and how ordinary people could use them to build wealth responsibly. Then I started learning and investing in the stock market, and I have now built over 10 years of experience in the financial market. All these and my understanding of technical analysis, is how I could turn $80 into $600 in just 4 months. Most of that money was made within the last 4 months of the trade.
